# 4. Agenda, Actions and the Budget

This chapter covers what the president does on purpose. Everything here exists to answer the handoff's question: *what are you actually trying to achieve with your presidency?*

## 4.1 The agenda

At the transition the player picks **three priorities from ten**. This is announced as the administration's Three-Point Agenda, and the papers publish a scorecard against it every anniversary.

Three is deliberate. A president who could pursue everything would face no trade-off. Three forces the player to decide what they are willing to neglect, and neglect has consequences (2.3).

### The ten reform tracks

| Track | Natural allies | Natural opponents | What neglecting it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power sector | Business, youth, manufacturers | Generator importers, distribution incumbents, tariff-payers at first | `gridFragility` climbs |
| Fiscal and revenue reform | Investors, finance ministry | Governors, importers, anyone newly taxed | Debt service squeezes everything |
| Security sector reform | The affected zones, civil society | Some security leadership, some governors | Insecurity spreads; `farmDistress` and `oilTheft` rise |
| Agriculture and food | Farm states, markets | Import licence holders | Food inflation, with a long lag |
| Education | Students, academics | The budget | The university storyline never ends |
| Health | Civil society, unions | The budget; medical tourism beneficiaries | Services decay; strikes |
| Infrastructure | Governors (for their states), business | Fiscal hawks; states that got nothing | Growth ceiling |
| Anti-corruption | Street, press, donors | Much of the Party and the Villa | `integrity` drifts down with every deal |
| Industry and jobs | Youth, manufacturers | Importers; trading partners | `jobs` stagnates |
| Public service reform | Nobody at first | Senior bureaucracy; everyone with a cousin on the payroll | `capacity` never improves |

Public service reform is the unglamorous one. It has no constituency, pays nothing for two years, and multiplies everything else. A player who picks it is making the bet the game is built to reward.

### Milestones

Each track has four or five milestones in sequence. A milestone names the **instrument** it needs.

Example, power sector:

| # | Milestone | Instrument | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Audit of sector debts published | Executive | Embarrasses previous beneficiaries |
| 2 | Cost-reflective tariff with protected band | Regulator + executive | Hardship up now; viability later |
| 3 | Gas payment guarantee | Budget line | Needs fiscal space |
| 4 | Electricity Act amendments (state markets) | Bill | Needs governors and both chambers |
| 5 | Transmission expansion commissioned | Legacy project | Years |

### Instruments and durability

| Instrument | Speed | Who can stop it | Durability (entrenchment) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Executive order or directive | 1 month | Courts; the next president | Fragile |
| Budget line | Annual cycle | The Assembly; execution rate | Lasts while funded |
| Appointment | 1 to 3 months | The Senate | Lasts while the person does |
| Regulator decision | 1 to 2 months | The regulator's independence; courts | Moderate |
| Federal-state compact | 3 to 6 months | Any governor who walks away | Moderate |
| Act of the National Assembly | 6 to 18 months | Committees, both chambers | Durable |
| Constitutional amendment | 2 years or more | Almost everyone | Near-permanent |

Each completed milestone records an **entrenchment** level: fragile, established or entrenched. Entrenchment rises with the instrument used, with time in force, and with whether the public has come to depend on it. It decides what survives the next president (8.3). A reform done quickly by order is a reform the successor can undo in an afternoon.

### Progress

A milestone advances when the player uses the **Push** action. Speed depends on the responsible minister's competence, `capacity`, and whether the required instrument is in place. A milestone needing a budget line does not move until October's budget funds it. A milestone needing a bill waits on the Assembly.

Each milestone, when pushed, can raise a **decision file**: the moment where the reform meets a real interest and the player chooses how hard to press. These files are where the agenda generates story.

### Off-agenda reform

The president can still act outside the three priorities through event options, but without milestones, without the scorecard and at higher PC cost. The agenda is a commitment device, not a cage.

At the mid-term (turn 24) the player may swap one priority. The papers call it a U-turn.

## 4.2 Presidential actions

One per month (two with a well-run Villa). This is the complete list.

| Action | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Push a milestone** | PC | Advances a reform track; may raise a decision file |
| **Lobby the Assembly** | PC | Improves a bill's chance of advancing this month |
| **Call in a governor or party figure** | PC, or a concession | Repairs a relationship; reveals what they want |
| **Reshuffle** | PC by clout | Appoint, dismiss, redeploy |
| **Tour a zone** | Time (the action) | Raises zone approval; lowers `sectionalGrievance`; larger effect if a local crisis is live and the president arrives early |
| **Address the nation** | PC | Shifts approval and press stance; effect depends on whether the facts support the speech |
| **Order an audit or investigation** | PC; relationships | Raises `integrity`; lowers `scandalHeat` later; may implicate allies |
| **Foreign trip** | Time | `standing`, `investorConfidence`; diminishing returns, and a press penalty if frequent during domestic crises |
| **Convene stakeholders** | None | Small PC recovery; small Establishment and Party gain. Achieves nothing concrete, and everyone leaves satisfied. |

"Convene stakeholders" is the do-nothing option, and it is a legitimate tactical choice when capital is low. It is also the joke.

Campaign actions (6.5) join this list from turn 40.

## 4.3 Institutional capacity as strategy

`capacity` and `integrity` touch almost every system. Collected in one place:

| System | Effect of high capacity and integrity |
|---|---|
| Budget execution | More of every naira becomes outcomes (2.5) |
| Information | Fresher, tighter statistics; system indices shown as numbers (2.4) |
| Adviser reads | Higher confidence, fewer "intelligence incomplete" |
| Reform speed | Milestones advance faster |
| Bill implementation | Signed laws deliver at full strength sooner |
| Candidate pools | Deeper bench; easier to balance zones without losing quality |
| Scandal hazard | Lower monthly risk; faster, cleaner resolution |
| Patronage | Lower cost of refusing debts (3.7) |
| Crises | More and better options appear on the same files |

The last row matters most for feel. The same grid-collapse file offers a president with a capable state the option "activate the restoration protocol; power back in 36 hours." A president without one gets "direct the Minister to investigate the remote and immediate causes."

The reverse holds. Each shortcut (a waived procurement process, an ignored court order, a loyalist put in charge of an agency) is a small permanent subtraction. No single one is fatal. Twenty of them produce a state that cannot tell the president the truth or carry out an instruction.

## 4.4 The budget

The budget is an annual ritual in October with real stakes and its own small drama.

### The envelope

```
fiscalSpace = revenue - debtService - wagesAndPensions - statutoryTransfers - subsidyBill
envelope    = fiscalSpace + borrowing
```

The Finance Minister presents the envelope. In the starting scenario, debt service and subsidy leave very little. The player's first budget is a lesson in why the previous governments did what they did.

### Borrowing

| Level | Effect |
|---|---|
| Prudent | Small envelope. `debtServiceRatio` falls slowly. Investors approve. |
| Moderate | Standard. Ratio roughly flat. |
| Heavy | Large envelope now. Ratio climbs; future envelopes shrink. Needs an Assembly resolution. |
| Central bank financing | Largest envelope. Feeds inflation directly. Requires a compliant central bank governor. |

### Allocation

Seven sectors, each funded at one of four levels:

**Security · Power and energy · Works · Agriculture · Education · Health · Social protection**

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Starved | Below maintenance. The index decays; pressures rise. |
| Lean | Holds roughly level |
| Adequate | Slow improvement |
| Priority | Fast improvement; required to fund most milestones in that sector |

Each level has a cost. The envelope never covers "Adequate" across the board at the start. Something is starved every year, and the player chooses what.

Social protection is the relief valve: funding it softens the hardship impact of painful reforms and slows `reformFatigue`. It is how a reformer buys time.

### Passage

After the player submits:

1. **Defence.** Ministers defend their budgets. Weak ministers lose money in committee.
2. **Insertions.** The Assembly adds constituency projects. A minor matter: accept (Party pleased, envelope shrinks, `integrity` dips), negotiate, or refuse and risk delay.
3. **Passage.** On time in December if the Party is Solid and the presiding officers are friendly. Otherwise it slips. A budget passed in March means a first quarter with no capital spending.
4. **Signature.** One of the ten or so signature moments.

### Supplementary budgets

Mid-year, an event can open a supplementary budget: for a windfall, a disaster, a wage award. It goes through the Assembly, which will have suggestions.

## 4.5 Legacy projects

Large physical projects that span years and often presidencies.

```ts
interface Project {
  id: string;
  name: string;              // "Lagos–Calabar Coastal Rail, Phase 1"
  sector: Sector;
  hostStates: StateId[];
  totalCost: number;         // ₦tn
  progress: number;          // 0 to 100
  officialProgress: number;  // what the ministry reports
  startedBy: PresidencyId;
  status: 'planned' | 'active' | 'stalled' | 'abandoned' | 'commissioned';
}
```

- A project needs funding every budget. A year at *Starved* in its sector stalls it.
- `officialProgress` and `progress` diverge when `integrity` is low. The refinery that has been 95% complete for some time is this mechanic.
- Commissioning a project is a ceremonial event, a PC windfall, a permanent index gain, and an entry on the legacy screen.
- **Inherited projects** are a recurring decision: finish the predecessor's project (cheap, effective, and they get the credit) or start one's own (expensive, slow, and it carries one's name). Abandoned projects stay on the map as abandoned projects.

A maximum of four active projects. Host states matter to zone approval, to governors and to the federal-character conversation.

## 4.6 Good fortune

Positive events (5.5) always pose a choice about use, never a free bonus.

An oil price spike: save it (reserves, `standing`, nobody thanks you), spend it on capital projects (growth later), or distribute it (approval now, nothing later, and the governors will insist). The windfall decision in a good year often does more to determine the legacy than any crisis in a bad one.
