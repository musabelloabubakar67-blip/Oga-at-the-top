# Connected acceptance histories

The eleven histories required by `docs/ACTION-PLAN.md`, with the executable evidence for each. `tests/experience/histories.check.ts` plays history 1 across three governments and fails if any check named below disappears.

| History | What must happen | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Industrial transformation across three governments | Power and a privileged deal create factories and a patron; a successor opens procurement and investigates; a third government inherits workers, firms and competing interests | `histories.check.ts`: the deal makes the industrialist owe the President; two plants stand at their sites; the second government inherits them and the deal (marked inherited), passes open contracting and charges the patron; the third inherits the plants, the open case, organised industrial workers and firms that want credit as constituencies | Verified |
| 2. The Reformer's Handover opening | Contested achievements, costly continuity, coalition pressure, deliberate team, a first consequential file | `dossiers.check.ts` (claims with verification, obligations, a working asset, leverage, a real first act); `opening.check.ts` (route, financier, constraint, programme and cabinet applied; every starting promise has a test) | Verified |
| 3. Refusal and settlement | A specific request is refused, closes, develops differently and settles on explicit bilateral terms | `requests.check.ts` (a refused request never returns unchanged; refusals by motive; partial settlement with a journalled residual; substitution needs agreement); `records.check.ts` (authored requests) | Verified |
| 4. A payroll liquidity crisis | Timed sales, tax-debt collection, savings and borrowing resolve or fail, with ownership and obligations accurate | `holdings.check.ts` (a salary crisis is met by timed measures; a sale is cash only when it settles, then the income goes); `money.check.ts` (conservation) | Verified |
| 5. A currency shock | Reserves constrain intervention; exposure revalues debt and investments; exports and imports feed the external account | `economy.check.ts` (a real fall enlarges the foreign debt and the fund abroad); `money.check.ts` (thin reserves, managed rate, fund returns); `bets.check.ts` (each asset changes its own dollar flows) | Verified |
| 6. A military corridor mission | Resources, intelligence, command, civilians, procurement and politics evolve over a year | `forces.check.ts` (a corridor campaign over a year; harm to civilians recorded and inherited); `military.check.ts` | Verified |
| 7. Exceptional recruitment under pressure | Conditions accepted, a distinctive capability, coherent reaction to breach | `recruitment.check.ts` (approach on every term, appoint, capability active; refused terms recorded); `tools/check-recruitment.ts` (breaches: a cut allocation resigns the mediator, a case instruction triggers the prosecutor's published resignation) | Verified |
| 8. Independent institution versus its founder | A justified adverse action, legal contest, continuing independence affecting founder and successor | `institutions.check.ts` (an agency charges its founder; the statistics bureau publishes; rules in force bind and survive; the court decides on the merits) | Verified |
| 9. Public services with competing approaches | University and health policies give access, quality and affordability outcomes with uneven beneficiaries | `society.check.ts` (services measured several ways; rival approaches succeed and fail differently; someone is left behind with the reason shown) | Verified |
| 10. Defeat and handover | Post-office choices, historical accountability, transfer to another human player | `succession.check.ts` (losing office opens decisions; an exported country can be inherited, altered files refused); `foundations.check.ts` (misconduct kept in the verdict) | Verified |
| 11. Succession earned through play | Preparation changes the transition and legacy without guaranteeing victory or control | `succession.check.ts` (preparation changes support and the survival forecast; heir, failed heir and rival inherit differently; a competent independent successor can succeed) | Verified |
